Why this test bench

What changed after the pilot

A climb made of many small steps

Chosen

The pilot's hill (circle packing) was solved in one window once the Coder had enough tokens, so it measured one turn, not a climb. Here every exercise needs four funded steps, so progress grows with the tokens each role actually gets.

Hand-offs at the refill

Phases

Work passed to the next role reaches it at the next window, like a daily hand-off. The pipeline fills (Researcher busy, others idle), runs, then drains (Reviewer busy). Fixed budgets leave tokens idle in both ends.

No budget below one call

Fair

Every role's budget covers its largest call seen in calibration, in every scenario, so no setup is stuck by construction.

Rules fixed before the first run

Pre-registered

Budget per window, summit and stop rules were set from calibration and checked in a zero-usage replay of all scenarios before any scenario ran.

Method

How to read this, and its limits

Run

    Measure

    • Windows to summit: first window whose shipped tests reach the summit
    • Expired: tokens left unused at a refill, as a share of tokens issued
    • Interruptions: agent-windows that ended with work waiting for lack of budget
    • Moved: tokens Rebbi lent between agents, from the shareable part of idle budgets
    • Every run's ledger passed rebbi check

    Limits

    • One model family and one task family
    • Hand-offs at the refill are a design choice, made explicit here
    • Medians over a handful of repeats: read differences of one or two windows as noise
    • Self-run, by the team that built Rebbi
